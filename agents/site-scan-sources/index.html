Patterns across most of the six

  • Tiered ladder, three or four rungs. Nearly every site has the same shape: a light advisory tier (hours per month, from roughly $2,750 to $4,500 or EUR 1 to 2k), an embedded fractional tier (1 to 2 days a week or 10 to 20 hours a week, $8k to $20k a month), and a fixed-scope project (audit, due diligence, sprint, $6.5k to $15k). Interim or full build sits on top as "let's talk".
  • Price anchoring against a full-time CTO is universal where prices are shown: "£200k salary", "$350K–600K/year", "EUR 15-20k/month + equity", "£150k+". Four of six put real numbers on the page; the two that do not (Razak, Sadde) still publish the market range or the pricing model.
  • "Best for" lines keyed to funding stage (pre-seed/seed, Series A, Series B+, PE-backed) rather than to problem type. Dostal and Schmidt both also give team-size bands.
  • Primary CTA is a free 30-minute call on five of six, with Calendly, cal.com or an unnamed tool. The exception (p0stman) replaces the call with a brief form and a promise of a written scope and price in 48 hours.
  • Proof is thin on quotes and heavy on counters and case outcomes. Three of six have zero testimonial quotes. Where quotes exist they are attributed with title and company, and Reyem links each to Clutch verification. Outcome-led case lines (throughput, money raised, contract won) are the common currency.
  • Disqualification copy is a recurring move. Schmidt ("you don't need me"), Sadde ("When NOT to Hire"), Reyem's advisory tier spelling out "no pager, no hands-on coding". Saying who it is not for reads as confidence.

Worth adapting for Learn with O.J.

  1. A named, fixed-price, fixed-duration entry engagement with a written deliverable, credited against the retainer. Reyem's "Technology Health Check" ($2,000, two weeks, scored report, 60-minute walkthrough, fee credited in month 3) is the cleanest entry point in the set and it removes the "discovery call or nothing" problem. Concrete suggestion: a one-week "Infra and Delivery Health Check" at a fixed price (roughly 6 to 8 hours at the $325 rate, so around $2,000 to $2,600), scored across five or six dimensions she actually audits (deploy pipeline, observability, on-call load, AI tooling adoption, hiring plan), delivered as a short written report plus a 60-minute readout, with the fee credited against the first month of any fractional engagement. Every retainer card's CTA on /showcase then becomes "Start with the Health Check".

  2. Productise the AI-assisted development training the way Razak does "The Shipyard". A name, a format ("2 days in-person" or "half-day remote"), a starting price, and a headcount cap ("from £5,000 for up to 6 people") turn a service description into something a founder can forward to their CTO. The Lucie/Guiltless story already proves the sales motion, so the page can name the offer, give "from $X for up to N people", list what the team walks out with, and point at the discovery call. Secondary idea from Dostal: a four-question self-service calculator (team size, stage, current bottleneck, hours lost) that outputs a number and ends on the booking link, no email gate.

Patterns that held across most sites

  • One offer, not a menu. Four of six sell a single named workshop. The ones with a catalog (Rentea, Artima) put the AI offer on its own page or domain.
  • The free call is the universal entry point. Every site that has any funnel at all routes to a 30-minute call (Notion Calendar, cal.com, Calendly, or plain email). Only one (getainative) puts a self-service step before the call.
  • Pricing is either a single flat number or a deliberately vague anchor. €1,000 flat (wmedia), €390/€490 per seat (Rentea), "Low four figures" (getainative), "less than sending the team to a decent conference" (ghuntley). Nobody in the solo set shows a per-seat-per-day grid. The agencies that were dropped (DataNorth €2,900/4h/10 people, claudeworkshop $1,000/person/day) are the ones with explicit grids.
  • Proof is thin and mostly reach-based. Newsletter counts, member counts, developers-trained counts, conference names. Only Rentea has named client testimonials. No one has a case study with a measured outcome.
  • Free material is the proof. ghuntley's free workshop repo, wmedia's Claude Code Tips, Copes's download library. The free thing demonstrates the teaching before the paid thing is sold.
  • Syllabus is shown as a module list, and the better pages separate "what we cover" from "what you leave with" (wmedia's "What comes out of the workshop", Rentea's "Wrap-up & Take-Aways", Zeo's starter artefacts list among the dropped sites).

Patterns across the six

  • Content site first, services one click away. Four of six (jhall, serverlessfirst, theburningmonk, nickjanetakis) lead with writing and a newsletter on the home page and keep the offer list on a "Hire Me" / "Services" / "Work Together" page. Only Mangoteque and ACL lead with the pitch.
  • Productized ladder with prices on the page is the norm for the stronger sites. jhall, serverlessfirst and theburningmonk all publish fixed prices and all three have the same shape: a cheap paid entry (call), a fixed-fee diagnostic (roadmap, review, audit, $6k to $7.5k), a bigger build or training, and an unpriced or "from" monthly retainer. Mangoteque and ACL (the two with the most senior buyers) publish no prices.
  • "You're a ..." situation lines instead of feature lists. serverlessfirst, jhall and theburningmonk each describe who the offer is for in second person before saying what it includes.
  • Intake is a form, not a calendar, for anything over about $1k. Google Forms (jhall), Typeform (theburningmonk), per-service "apply" pages (serverlessfirst), contact form (ACL). Calendar tools (SavvyCal, Calendly) are used only for the low-priced paid call. Nobody offers a free call on the page.
  • Testimonials carry name, role and company, and are placed next to the offer they relate to, not only on a wall. Quantified outcomes are rare (one "4 weeks" and one "3 times faster" across all six). Logos appear on the three with bigger clients.
  • Lead magnets are email courses or gated PDFs, never audits or quizzes. 5-day and 9-lesson email courses (serverlessfirst, jhall) with an email-only form, two gated PDFs (Mangoteque), weekly newsletters (theburningmonk, nickjanetakis). No free assessment, quiz or scorecard on any of the six.
